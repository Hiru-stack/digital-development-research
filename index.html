Beyond Connectivity

A Comparative Analysis of Digital Access, Internet Use and Digital Capability in Sri Lanka and Selected Asian Countries

Research overview

Research problem

Sri Lanka has expanded digital connectivity, but access to connectivity does not automatically mean that people use the Internet or have strong computer skills. This study examines the difference between connectivity, actual Internet use and digital capability.

Main research question

Are improvements in Sri Lanka's digital connectivity being matched by improvements in Internet use, computer literacy and digital capability?

Novelty of the study

  • Combines detailed Sri Lankan DCS computer/digital literacy statistics with standardized ITU international connectivity indicators.
  • Does not treat connectivity and capability as the same thing; it compares broadband subscriptions with actual Internet use and literacy.
  • Measures the widening difference between digital literacy and computer literacy in Sri Lanka.
  • Benchmarks Sri Lanka against Bangladesh, India, Indonesia and Malaysia using a common international dataset.
  • Highlights a possible connectivity–capability gap: relatively strong broadband subscription rankings alongside lower Internet use.

Data and methodology

Data sources

Department of Census and Statistics, Sri Lanka: Computer Literacy Statistics annual bulletins, 2014–2025.

International Telecommunication Union: ICT indicators for selected Asian countries. The main country-to-country comparison uses 2014–2024 because 2025 has missing observations for some indicators.

Indicators used

Computer literacy, digital literacy, household desktop/laptop ownership, individuals using the Internet, active mobile-broadband subscriptions per 100 inhabitants and fixed-broadband subscriptions per 100 inhabitants.

Correlation results

Internet use ↔ mobile broadband: 0.90

Internet use ↔ fixed broadband: 0.73

Mobile broadband ↔ fixed broadband: 0.68

The strongest observed association is between mobile-broadband subscriptions and Internet use. However, the pooled result includes both differences across countries and changes over time, so it should not be interpreted as causal.

Preliminary conclusion

The analysis suggests that Sri Lanka's digital development cannot be understood through connectivity measures alone. Broadband subscriptions have expanded and digital literacy has increased strongly, yet household computer ownership has remained nearly unchanged and Sri Lanka's 2024 Internet-use rate is lower than the selected comparison countries.

The study therefore identifies a connectivity–capability gap as a useful way to understand Sri Lanka's digital development. The next stage of the research should test the robustness of this finding using country-level trend analysis and, where available, Sri Lankan demographic or sector-level literacy differences.

Research objectives

  1. Analyze changes in computer literacy, digital literacy and household computer ownership in Sri Lanka.
  2. Compare Sri Lanka's Internet use and broadband connectivity with selected Asian countries.
  3. Examine whether improved connectivity is accompanied by comparable improvements in Internet use and digital capability.
  4. Identify the main digital-development gaps that may require attention in future digital-inclusion policy and research.

Limitations

  • The DCS and ITU datasets measure different but related concepts; indicators are compared conceptually rather than merged as if they were identical.
  • The international sample is limited to five countries with sufficiently comparable observations in the extracted dataset.
  • Some 2025 international observations are missing, so the main international comparison ends in 2024.
  • Aggregated secondary data cannot establish individual-level or causal relationships.
